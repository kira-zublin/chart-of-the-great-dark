# Coriolis location art direction

Use this guide for original location illustrations in the Chart of the Great Dark prototype. Ground each image in the relevant Core Rules location and faction descriptions. The local Core Rules PDF and `assets/ship-city-map.jpg` are references for geography, material, atmosphere, and painterly treatment; do not trace, reproduce, or closely restage published art.

## The world to depict

- **People:** Ship City's inhabitants are human. Show varied ages, faces, skin tones, clothing, and occupations. Garuda are distinctive birds and may appear where the book places them; other nonhuman creatures require a specific location brief. Background crowds must not turn into animal-headed or alien humanoids.
- **City:** A crowded asteroid metropolis assembled from old ship hulls, cut stone, gantries, pressure doors, cable cages, carved caverns, gardens, and hard-used machines. Technology is repaired, repurposed, and scarce. The city is alive with food, trade, music, murals, and domestic details despite that scarcity.
- **Factions:** Navigators favor star charts, mathematical instruments, observatories, and measured grandeur. Machinists combine heavy machinery with angular geometric murals and strong color. Gardeners bring tended, sometimes overgrown life into caves and public spaces. Explorers use practical, battered expedition equipment and crowded work areas. Let these cues serve the named place rather than adding every faction to every image.
- **Mood:** Wonder and unease coexist. Use darkness, fog, and the Great Dark as atmosphere while preserving readable landmarks and inviting spaces for play.
- **Light and enclosure:** Ship City is inside and around an asteroid, not under an ordinary open sky. Show cavern roofs, ship hull ceilings, pressure structures, or the visible frame of a dome. Beyond a dome, the backdrop is the Great Dark and stars. Let light globes, navigation lamps, candles, work lights, signs of machinery, and occasional filtered starlight define small pools of light. Any Jumuah light must be specifically justified by the location and should never turn a scene into a bright terrestrial afternoon. Preserve enough local contrast to read the art at game size.

## Painterly language

Aim for an **original watercolor-led book illustration**: broad translucent watercolor washes, pigment blooms, paper showing through, wet-on-wet transitions, expressive loose brushwork, and softened or broken edges.  Soften shapes, add paint flecks, and make it gritty and distressed with micrographic noise.  Stylistic inspirations are Martin Grip and Jaime Jones. Use sparse ink-like drawing to anchor essential architecture. Keep crisp detail only at one focal point; describe crowds, equipment, masonry, and distant buildings with grouped shapes and suggestive marks rather than individually rendered objects. Gouache can supply a few opaque highlights, but should not make the whole scene dense or polished. Favor a composed illustration over a camera-like rendering. Use muted weathered blue and green, charcoal, muted red, and small ochre or brass accents where the place calls for them. Dark values may dominate; practical local lights create restrained warm islands within them.

Useful prompt phrases: `watercolor wash is the dominant medium`, `translucent layered pigment`, `pigment blooms and backruns`, `warm paper visible between shapes`, `soft lost edges`, `sparse ink for essential structure`, `fewer, broader shapes`, `one selectively detailed focal point`, `human-scale lived-in space`, `small pools of practical light`, `stars beyond a framed dome`. `paint flecks`

## Reusable prompt attachment

Append and adapt this to each location-specific prompt:

> Original atmospheric watercolor book illustration of this specific Coriolis location. Human inhabitants only unless the location brief explicitly names a nonhuman creature; Garuda are birds, not humanoids. Built from weathered stone, reused ship hulls, brass, worn metal, fabric, and repaired machinery. Watercolor is the dominant medium: broad translucent washes, pigment blooms, visible paper, loose expressive marks, soft lost edges, paint flecks, and sparse ink for necessary structure. Suggest detail rather than rendering every surface or object. Ship City is an enclosed asteroid city: show cavern or hull ceilings, or a framed dome with stars and the Great Dark beyond. Use globes, navigation lamps, candles, machinery, or filtered starlight for local illumination; no ordinary blue sky, daylight, desert sun, or sunlit sand palette. Preserve the location's geography, function, and Guild identity from the brief. No text, labels, symbols, interface, or watermark.

Add the relevant composition clause:

- **Hub map:** `Wide oblique or cutaway illustrated map with coherent routes and clearly separated landmarks; leave usable spaces for clickable markers. No drawn grid.`
- **Vista:** `Wide scene with a readable open foreground for character standups and a clear human-scale focal point.`
- **Explorable:** `Strict overhead orthographic floor plan with legible walking surfaces, walls, obstacles, entrances, and room boundaries that can align with the specified grid. No perspective view or figures occupying playable squares.`
- **Card illustration:** `Crop-friendly focal point that remains clear in a narrow, shallow panel.`

## Avoid

Do not use `cinematic photorealism`, glossy 3D game-concept rendering, chrome-clean spaceships, generic cyberpunk neon, pristine high-tech interiors, military science-fiction uniforms everywhere, or medieval-fantasy castles. Avoid Star Wars-style cantina aliens, animal-headed people, pointed ears, tentacles, extra limbs, and invented humanoid species in Ship City. Avoid visual clutter that obscures routes, room edges, player standups, or map markers. Do not invent a new skyline or place landmarks in relationships that contradict the book.

Reject open blue skies, desert horizons, harsh overhead sun, long sunny outdoor shadows, and a uniformly golden or dusty-sand color cast. Reject painstakingly rendered crowds, individually described bricks, and glossy polished machine surfaces when they overwhelm the watercolor wash. A painted scene may remain legible while leaving much of its space quiet and unresolved.

After generation, inspect the image at its intended UI size. Reject or revise it if the inhabitants look nonhuman, the style looks photographic or 3D, the place cannot be recognized from its brief, interactive space is unclear, the illumination implies an outdoor desert day, or fine detail overwhelms the watercolor treatment. Record useful prompt adjustments here before producing more assets.
